# Fact-check log: footnotes-gone-feral (adult)

Checked 2026-11-09 by an independent checker (one-subject version of the deck), using WebSearch/WebFetch on History.com, Merriam-Webster, NPS, the Miller Center, the White House Historical Association, the Historical Society of the New York Courts, Wikipedia, the Texas Historical Commission, Smithsonian Magazine, swissinfo.ch, arthurconandoyle.co.uk, Mental Floss, HistoryNet and UK Parliament.

## Changes
- fgf01: "first president born a citizen of the United States" can be argued, since there was no national citizenship before 1789, and History.com words it as "the first not born a British subject". Changed to "[was the first president born after the Declaration of Independence]". History.com: born 1782, and all seven earlier presidents were born before 1776. Confirmed: Dutch as his first language, and the last sitting VP elected until 1988 (History.com). OK dates from an 1839 Boston Morning Post joke, and the Old Kinderhook story is a myth (Merriam-Webster).
- fgf02: no text change. Garfield addressed German immigrants partly in their language on Oct 16, 1880 (NPS). Mules on a canal boat at 16, the 36th ballot, and the 1876 proof as a congressman (Wikiwand/Wikipedia).
- fgf03: "helped throw his college's bell into the Erie Canal" rests only on Mental Floss, and the story is usually told as "reputed". Replaced with "[had Louis Comfort Tiffany's design firm redo rooms in the White House]" (WHHA: Arthur commissioned Associated Artists, Tiffany's firm). The Jennings claim is accurate as worded: the case was assigned to junior partner Arthur, the jury found for Jennings, and the Third Avenue Railroad agreed to desegregate its cars at once (NY Courts Historical Society; Miller Center). All New York transit followed by 1861, so "a streetcar line" is not overstated. Real: dropped "1829" (not confirmed here) and kept the rumor framing (Mental Floss: born in Vermont despite rumors). 24 wagonloads to auction (WHHA).
- fgf04: "while the public thought he was fishing" rested only on potus.com. Changed to "[had secret surgery in a chair tied to the mast of a friend's yacht]" (Miller Center). Confirmed: White House wedding in 1886 (Miller Center); mayor of Buffalo 1882; 414 first-term vetoes against 204 for all earlier presidents (potus.com).
- fgf05: replaced the mirror source loki.editorial.aetnd.com with Mental Floss and HistoryNet (1877, phone number 1). Confirmed: the cat came from David Sickels, a US diplomat in Bangkok, in 1878 (Carl Anthony). The oath was taken in the Red Room (Google Arts & Culture). The egg roll was banned at the Capitol by 1876 and moved to the White House in 1878 (The Reconstruction Era).
- fgf06: "never saw combat in 35 years as a soldier" could be argued for the Supreme Allied Commander, and the 35 is unsourced. Changed to "[was kept home training troops in World War I despite asking to go overseas]" (History.com). Confirmed: birth name David Dwight (History.com); born in Denison, Texas, Oct 14, 1890, and back in Kansas at about 18 months (Texas Historical Commission); squirrels trapped and moved to Rock Creek Park (History.com).
- fgf07: "pardoned the police officer" changed to "pardoned the officer". Armand Barbier was a special officer of a privately paid patrol (Wikipedia). "Money he printed himself" changed to "money he issued himself", since his scrip was printed for him. Confirmed: 1872 bridge decrees, Emperor from Sept 17, 1859, and Protector of Mexico by early 1866 (Wikipedia).
- fgf08: "helped make skiing popular in England" was inaccurate. Changed to "[helped make skiing popular in Switzerland]": swissinfo says he learned in Davos in 1894 and helped popularise skiing there, though he didn't introduce it. Whaling source upgraded to Smithsonian Magazine (surgeon on the Hope at 20, 1880). Knighted Oct 24, 1902, for his Boer War defense (arthurconandoyle.co.uk). Cottingley Fairies confirmed (Wikipedia).
- fgf09: the cap "first cookbook at 29" was too easy. Changed to "at 39" (Mastering the Art of French Cooking, 1961; born Aug 15, 1912, so 49). Real now says 49. Too tall for the WACs or WAVES, OSS shark repellent, and Dr. Juliet Bleeb in We're Back! (Wikipedia).
- fgf10: no change. Lyman Cutlar, an American, shot a Hudson's Bay Company pig on June 15, 1859. The pig was the only casualty, joint occupation lasted 12 years, and Kaiser Wilhelm I's award came Oct 21, 1872 (NPS).
- fgf11: real said Louis XIV "bought it as the French Blue", but he bought the Tavernier stone and had it cut into the French Blue. Changed to "Tavernier bought it there in 1666 and sold it to Louis XIV of France" (Wikipedia). Stolen 1792, red phosphorescence, registered mail in brown paper (Wikipedia; Postal Museum).
- fgf12: replaced the test host webtest.parliament.uk with UK Parliament's facts pages (Big Ben is the Great Bell; pre-decimal pennies regulate the clock) and History.com (cracked two months after first ringing on July 11, 1859, silent for upwards of four years; renamed Elizabeth Tower in 2012).
