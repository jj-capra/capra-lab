# timeline-glitch: fact-check log

Checked by an independent checker on 2026-11-09 against DECK_SPEC.md and CHECK_SPEC.md (one-subject version of the deck).

## Changes

- tg01 Cleopatra: "was living in Rome when Caesar was assassinated" is true but loose (she was visiting and staying at Caesar's villa) → "was staying in Rome". Wikipedia: "Cleopatra stayed in Rome until about mid-April" 44 BC. Also checked: reign 51 to 30 BC (21 years), died at 39 (the cap is false); born 69 BC, 254 years after Alexander died in 323 BC; WorldAtlas has the pyramid around 2560 BC and the Moon landing in 1969.
- tg02 Oxford University: `real` said "Henry VIII wasn't born until 1491", which no source in the card checked → "long before Henry VIII's time". BestColleges: Henry III's charter, 1248 (the cap is false). Added Britannica (Tenochtitlan founded c. 1325) to the teaching claim (ox.ac.uk: teaching "in some form in 1096"). Wikipedia says the Oxford scholars joined some already in Cambridge in 1209 → "leave and help start Cambridge University". Women were first admitted to degrees on 14 October 1920 (Wikipedia), after WWI.
- tg03 Hubble: the cap "took the first photo ever of a black hole, in 2019" was the only claim with a number and with "ever" → "took the first picture of a black hole while orbiting Earth" (`real` still gives the EHT and 2019). Cleared the NOT WEB-CHECKED flag: ESA says Hubble launched April 24, 1990, on Discovery; Engadget says the first website went live at CERN on December 20, 1990 (public in August 1991, so "went online" is the accurate wording); HISTORY.com (A&E's editorial host) says Google filed for incorporation September 4, 1998. NASA: the mirror's edge was ground too flat by 2.2 microns. MIT Technology Review: the first black hole image came from the Event Horizon Telescope, April 10, 2019.
- tg04 Great Pyramid of Giza: "was built while woolly mammoths were still alive" repeats a fact already on the kids Woolly mammoths card (wait-when) → replaced with "was already more than 2,000 years old when Alexander the Great was born". Wikipedia: pyramid c. 2600 BC, with Khufu's reign estimated at 2700 to 2500 BC; Alexander born 356 BC, so at least 2,144 years. The old Business Insider source is gone. Wikipedia confirms the rest: tallest for "more than 3,700 years", built over about 26 years (Herodotus says 20, so the 90-year cap is false), and work-gang graffiti in the relieving chambers.
- tg05 Tutankhamun: the cap "first royal tomb ever found" was the only claim with "ever" → "first royal tomb found". Wikipedia (Valley of the Kings): Seti I's tomb was found in 1817 and several were open since antiquity. BBC formed Oct 18, 1922, first broadcast weeks later; tomb found Nov 1922 (both Wikipedia). Reign c. 1332 to 1323 BC, at least 1,170 years after the pyramid; became king between 8 and 9.
- tg06 Colosseum: "already 1,000 years old when Oxford started teaching" rested on Oxford's earliest evidence (1096) with only a 16-year margin, and Oxford says there is "no clear date of foundation" → "was already more than 1,100 years old when the Magna Carta was sealed". Britannica gives AD 80 and Wikipedia gives June 15, 1215: 1,135 years. Britannica confirms the rest: "hundreds of Roman sailors" worked the velarium; Titus dedicated it in 80 with 100 days of games after work began in 70 to 72 under Vespasian, so the Nero cap is false. Caesar died in 44 BC, 123 years before.
- tg07 Leif Erikson: the cap "grew up in Norway" is plainly false. World History Encyclopedia: probably born in Iceland around 970 to 980; Erik founded the Greenland settlement c. 985, "where young Leif grew up"; Leif sailed to Norway later, as "a promising young man" serving Olaf Tryggvason. `real` now adds that he sailed to Norway as a young man, so a player who knows of his Norway trip sees why the cap is still false. Added HISTORY.com for William's landing (September 28, 1066) against Leif's c. 1000.
- tg08 Leonardo da Vinci: cleared the NOT WEB-CHECKED flag. HISTORY.com: Joan of Arc burned May 30, 1431; Wikipedia: Leonardo born April 15, 1452 (the cap is false by 21 years). Mona Lisa begun by October 1503, after Columbus's 1492 voyage (Britannica); robot designed late 15th century, prototype around 1495; stolen Aug 21, 1911, found Dec 1913.
- tg09 Benjamin Franklin: sources only. Newton died March 1727 and Franklin was born January 1706, so they overlapped 21 years. Franklin was an honored guest at the crewed Tuileries balloon flight of Dec 1, 1783, and he "never patented his inventions" (all Wikipedia). Added the National Archives (Washington sworn in April 30, 1789) against Franklin's death on April 17, 1790.
- tg10 Orville Wright: the cap "made his first flight while Queen Victoria was still alive" repeats the adult Queen Victoria card (calendar-whiplash, "still on the throne when the Wright brothers..."), and it sat next to a word-for-word twin ("made his famous first flight while Harriet Tubman was still alive"), which made the card a 50/50. → New cap: "was born while Abraham Lincoln was still president". NPS: born August 19, 1871; PBS: Lincoln died April 15, 1865. "Lived long enough to see a plane fly faster than sound" was shaky: HISTORY.com says Yeager's Oct 14, 1947 flight stayed secret until June 1948, after Orville died (Jan 30, 1948, NPS). → "was still alive when a plane first flew faster than sound". Cleared the 12-second flag (Wikipedia, Wright Flyer: "lasted 12 seconds", 120 feet, Orville at the controls). Tubman died in 1913, ten years after the flight.
- tg11 Sputnik 1: sources only. Oct 4, 1957 (Air Force Museum, Centennial of Flight); NASA created in 1958; a "23-inch-diameter" capsule; iPhone June 29, 2007; flight Dec 17, 1903. The cap is false: 53 years 9 months after the first flight, 49 years 9 months before the iPhone. The Moon landing on July 20, 1969 (WorldAtlas) came 11 years 9 months later.
- tg12 Apollo 11: the cap "touched down while a Civil War veteran was still alive" is arguable. Albert Woolson (d. 1956) was the last verified veteran, but unverified claimants lived on (Sylvester Magee claimed Union service and died in 1971; from memory, not web-checked, and moot once the cap was replaced). → New cap: "beamed its moonwalk to TV sets around the world in full color". Smithsonian Air & Space: the first lunar-surface TV was black and white, and only the command-module broadcasts were in color; CSIRO Parkes: a black-and-white slow-scan camera. Also "landed before the first home video game console went on sale" repeats the kids Moon card (wait-when) → "touched down on the Moon before the first message was sent over the internet". CBS: the ARPANET "lo" message, Oct 29, 1969, three months after July 20, 1969. That stays true even if you date the internet later. Customs form (Space.com: Honolulu, July 24, 1969, "authentic") and the 66 years (WorldAtlas: Dec 17, 1903 to July 20, 1969 is 65 years 7 months) both check out.
