## Changes

- sb02 Minecraft: the cap "came out before the very first version of Roblox" was the only claim with "very" → "the first version of Roblox". Pocket Gamer says Roblox launched September 1, 2006, and Minefort says Minecraft's first public alpha came May 17, 2009 under the name Cave Game, so the cap is false. The creeper source (Wikipedia, which wouldn't open here) → the V&A museum page: "created accidentally by Markus 'Notch' Persson, as he wrongly programmed the code to create a pig". Mojang: Microsoft agreed to pay 2.5 billion dollars (Sept 2014).
- sb05 The first iPhone: sources only. Wikipedia: released June 29, 2007, with no App Store (2008), no copy and paste (2009) and only a rear camera. MacRumors: the App Store opened July 10, 2008, so the cap is false. Spotify launched October 7, 2008, with no earlier public beta in Wikipedia, so "more than a year before" holds.
- sb07 New Horizons: the cap "found out up close that Pluto has no moons at all" was too easy and the only claim with an absolute ("at all") → "discovered Pluto's biggest moon, Charon, when it flew past". NASA: Charon was discovered in June 1978 by James Christy and Robert Harrington, from Earth, and is the largest of five moons. Cleared the NOT WEB-CHECKED flag and replaced the light sources with Britannica: launched January 19, 2006, closest approach July 14, 2015 (9.5 years), "a large, young, heart-shaped region of ice". Space.com: the heart is Tombaugh Regio. The IAU demoted Pluto in August 2006, months after launch (Universe Magazine).
- sb08 Michael Phelps: "goes by the nickname the Flying Fish" → "got the nicknames the Flying Fish and the Baltimore Bullet" (past tense for a retired swimmer; Wikipedia lists both). The Wikipedia mirror issue: the live en.wikipedia.org page now opens, and the medal claims moved to Olympics.com: 28 medals (23 gold, 3 silver, 2 bronze), eight golds at Beijing 2008, retired after Rio 2016. No record claim, so nothing can go stale.
- sb10 The James Webb Space Telescope: Good Morning America replaced where possible. Space.com: the sunshield is "the size of a tennis court", in orbit at the second Lagrange point, "1 million miles" out, so the cap is false. Wikipedia: launched December 25, 2021. GMA kept for the 10-billion-dollar cost.
- sb11 The Tokyo 2020 Olympics: the cap "first Olympics ever held in Japan" was the only claim with "ever" → "the first Olympics, summer or winter, held in Japan". Wikipedia: the fourth in Japan, after 1964, 1972 and 1998. The card stays light: the only pandemic-era fact is the plain one that it ran in 2021 under the Tokyo 2020 name. Medals: EcoWatch says they were made from donated phones and laptops (Wikipedia confirms the phone drive). Olympics.com confirms both skaters were 13 (Nishiya gold, Leal silver), which CBS only implied.
- sb12 The Curiosity rover: "leaves the letters JPL in Morse code in its wheel tracks" → "has wheels that stamp the letters JPL in Morse code into the dirt", which stays true whatever the rover's status (JPL: Morse code for JPL on all six wheels). Space.com: "powered by a nuclear battery" (the cap is false); the sky crane lowered it on cables; landed August 6, 2012, in Gale Crater. Nextgov: the birthday tune came in August 2013.
