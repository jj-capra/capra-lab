Checked 2026-11-09 by an independent checker (17 web searches shared across the three high school decks,
plus fetches of Wikipedia, Britannica, NPS, Founders Online, NTIA, the White House Historical Association,
Smithsonian, Live Science and History.com). Every claim was confirmed online, including the reused ones the
writer had not re-read (Adams and Jefferson dying the same day, Coolidge's raccoon, all four Napoleon

## Changes
- hn01: real said "the unfinished White House", which no fetched page says → "the newly built White House". Sources now name the facts: White House (Adams first to take residence, Nov 1, 1800), History.com (Adams already headed home as Jefferson was sworn in, March 4, 1801; both died July 4, 1826).
- hn02: "suggested hiring Portugal's navy to fight pirates" rested only on Mental Floss → confirmed in Madison's own words at Founders Online (House debate, Feb 1794: he would "hire the Portuguese ships of war" against the Algerine corsairs instead of building a fleet). Kept.
- hn03: real added "strong in math and science but weak in military subjects", which no fetched page supports → "right in the middle of the pack" (NPS: 21st of 39, class of 1843). The speeding arrest stays worded "was arrested": Smithsonian says the arrest "isn't in dispute" (DC police chief Cathy Lanier, 2012; officer William West's 1908 account gives the detail).
- hn04: "first president to appear on television" is arguable (Herbert Hoover was on TV in 1927, before he was president) → "[was the first sitting president to appear on TV]" (NTIA: "the first appearance on television by a sitting president", April 30, 1939). The cap now mirrors it: "[was the first sitting president to speak on the radio]". It's plainly false: Harding spoke over radio in 1922 (Harding Presidential Sites) and Coolidge's Dec 6, 1923 address was broadcast (Wikipedia). Sources disagree on which of the two was first, so real names both and says neither was first: "Presidents were on the radio years before FDR: Warren Harding in 1922 and Calvin Coolidge in 1923. FDR made radio famous with his fireside chats." Worldatlas swapped for NTIA.
- hn08: "stood about 5 foot 6, taller than the average Frenchman then" is contested (How It Works says above average; Wikipedia's Napoleon gives no height) → "[was born a year after Genoa handed his home island, Corsica, to France]" (Wikipedia: born 1769, one year after Genoa ceded Corsica by the 1768 Treaty of Versailles). The other three were rechecked: Clisson et Eugenie, 1795 (emperor 1804). Sphinx: the cannonball is a legend, and Norden drew it noseless in 1737 (Smithsonian Journeys). Oldest surviving photograph 1827; Napoleon died 5 May 1821.
- hn09: sources now name the facts. Seven inmates on 14 July 1789. Decimal time: 1793 decree, suspended 1795. Metric system devised in the early Revolution, launched 1799. Washington inaugurated April 30, 1789.
- hn11: "stood about 6 feet 8 inches tall" is only "reportedly" on Wikipedia → "[became tsar at age 10, sharing the throne with his half-brother]" (Wikipedia: chosen tsar at 10; ruled jointly with Ivan V until 1696). "with a razor" isn't in the source → "[personally shaved the beards off his own party guests]" (Smithsonian: "personally shave the beards from his horrified guests" at a reception). The mdwiki.org copy was swapped for en.wikipedia.org (New Year moved from 1 Sept to 1 Jan in 1699; four months at a Dutch East India Company yard).
- hn12: "never met her father" is arguable (Byron lived with baby Ada for her first weeks) → "[grew up without ever knowing her father, the poet Lord Byron]" (Britannica). Sources now name the fact on each claim (1843 translation and notes; only a small piece of the engine built; musical notes).
- Sources tightened on hn05, hn06, hn07 and hn10.
- Confirmed unchanged: Madison 5 ft 4, the 5,000 dollar bill, and beating Monroe in 1789. Grant born Hiram Ulysses; signed Yellowstone March 1, 1872. Thanksgiving moved Nov 30 to Nov 23, 1939, for merchants. Fala is the only presidential pet with a statue at the memorial.
