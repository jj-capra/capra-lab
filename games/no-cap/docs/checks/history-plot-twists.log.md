# history-plot-twists: fact-check log

Checked by an independent checker on 2026-11-09 against DECK_SPEC.md and CHECK_SPEC.md (one-subject version of the deck).

## Changes

- hp01 Thomas Jefferson: two claims repeated the high John Adams card (history-has-no-chill), which says Adams "was the first president to live in the White House" and "died on the very same day as" Jefferson. → New cap "served as the first vice president, under George Washington": Wikipedia says Jefferson was the first secretary of state (1790 to 1793) and vice president 1797 to 1801, under Adams. New fact "was the first president sworn in at Washington, D.C.": the Senate's inaugural committee lists March 4, 1801 as the "First inauguration in Washington, D.C." The mockingbird (Wikipedia, Dick the Mockingbird) and the books ("nearly 6,500 volumes" sold after the British burned the Library of Congress, Wikipedia) are confirmed.
- hp03 William Howard Taft: the cap is plainly false as worded. Wikipedia (White House): the West Wing burned on Christmas Eve 1929, under Hoover; Taft expanded the West Wing and created the first Oval Office in 1909, with no fire involved. `real` now gives both years so the reveal is clear. "Became the first president to throw out a baseball first pitch" → "began the tradition of presidents throwing out baseball's first pitch, in 1910", which is Wikipedia's own wording. Chief justice in 1921 (Wikipedia); Pauline Wayne grazed the White House lawn (Live Science).
- hp04 Herbert Hoover: sources upgraded. The 2012 archive copy and Mental Floss → History.com (born in West Branch, Iowa, "the first president born west of the Mississippi"; "a member of Stanford University's inaugural class", 1891), plus live Wikipedia (Pioneer Class, born August 10, 1874). Meridian International Center added for the Chinese ("the Hoovers spoke Chinese when they wanted to have a private conversation in the White House"), next to the Hoover-Minthorn House museum. Smithsonian kept for the desk phone.
- hp05 Christopher Columbus: sources only. Britannica says the Santa María ran aground on December 25, 1492 but doesn't name the ship home → added History.com: "Columbus sailed back to Spain on the Niña". Britannica also confirms: in irons in 1500, the predicted lunar eclipse on Jamaica, and "always maintained that he had found the true Indies and Cathay".
- hp07 Lewis and Clark: Wikipedia says the November 1805 vote was on moving the camp to the south side of the Columbia, not "where to spend the winter" → "vote on where to move their camp". Wikipedia confirms Mackenzie crossed to the Pacific in 1792 to 1793 (the cap is false), Seaman, and the 193 pounds of portable soup.
- hp08 Julius Caesar: the cap "was the very first Roman emperor" was the only claim with "very" → "was the first emperor of ancient Rome". `real` gave 27 BC for Augustus, which no source here checked → "after Caesar died". Wikipedia confirms the pirates (ransomed), July (renamed Julius), dictator, never emperor, and Octavian as "first emperor". The Julian calendar page confirms the 445-day year 46 BC.
- hp09 Alexander the Great: `real` put Pella "north of Greece", which reads as wrong (Pella is in Greece today and Macedon was a Greek kingdom) → "the capital of the kingdom of Macedon. Athens was a separate city-state far to the south." Wikipedia confirms born in Pella in 356 BC, tutored by Aristotle until 16, Bucephala, and undefeated in battle.
- hp10 Thomas Edison: sources were already live en.wikipedia.org (not a mirror); I added what each confirms. The Henry Ford: vote recorder patent in 1869. Wikipedia: hearing problems at 12, 1,093 US patents, and "many incandescent lamps had been devised by inventors prior to Edison" (the cap is false).
- hp11 Alexander Graham Bell: the cap "made his very first phone call to his mother" was the only claim with "very" → "made his first telephone call to his mother". `real` said "the next room"; the Library of Congress notebook page says "another room" (Mental Floss says "down the hall") → "in another room", dated March 10, 1876. The LOC page is an End of Term web-archive copy of memory.loc.gov, now backed by Mental Floss. Ahoy (Wikipedia), the HD-4 at 70.8 mph in 1919 (Britannica) and the 1880 photophone (Mental Floss) all check out.

## Checked, no change

- hp02 John Quincy Adams (Wikipedia): diary from 1779 until 1848; secretary to Francis Dana in St. Petersburg in 1781 (age 14, born July 1767); House from 1831 to 1848 (17 years). Jackson won 99 electoral votes to Adams's 84 in 1824, so the cap is false.
- hp06 Marco Polo (Wikipedia): returned to Venice in 1295 "after 24 years"; dictated his travels to fellow prisoner Rustichello; Columbus owned an annotated copy. For the cap, Lapham's Quarterly has a Genoa soldier's 1279 inventory with a basket of macaroni and calls the Polo legend "utterly spurious".
- hp12 The ancient Olympic Games: 776 BC to AD 393, every four years (Wikipedia); Guinness: Koroibos "was a cook from the nearby city of Elis"; Cynisca of Sparta was the first woman listed as an Olympic victor (chariot owner). The cap is false: the longest ancient foot race was the dolichos (about 7.5 to 9 km), and Wikipedia credits Michel Bréal with conceiving the 1896 marathon.
