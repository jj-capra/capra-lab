# totally-true-history: fact-check log (kids)

Second check (Nov 9 2026), on the one-subject version of the deck. Every claim was checked against the cited page or a new one.

- th01 George Washington: the donkey was cited to a page that only says "a stud" → Mount Vernon's Royal Gift entry (a jack from King Charles III of Spain, 1784 to 1785, bred with mares for mules). Confirmed: one tooth left at the 1789 inauguration (Mount Vernon); Adams first moved in Nov 1, 1800 (Wikipedia White House); surveyor oath July 20, 1749, at 17 (Wikipedia).
- th02 Abraham Lincoln: "is honored in the National Wrestling Hall of Fame" is rated "Mixture" by Snopes (an Outstanding American award in 1992, not an induction) → "got an award from the National Wrestling Hall of Fame". Darwin Feb 12, 1809 (Mental Floss); patent 6,469, 1849 (Smithsonian); no recording exists, and he died 12 years before the phonograph (Smithsonian).
- th03 Theodore Roosevelt: "the first person from outside Europe to win a Nobel Prize" is arguable (Ronald Ross, 1902 Medicine, was born in India) → "the first American to win a Nobel Prize" (Smithsonian, 1906). The submarine source was a weak list (potus.com) → Naval History and Heritage Command ("In August 1905... the first U.S. President to dive in a submarine", Plunger). Cap confirmed: his flight was Oct 1910, after he left office (Flight Journal), and FDR was the first to fly in office (potus.com). Emily Spinach confirmed (WHHA).
- th04 Mount Rushmore: the Hall of Records is an unfinished tunnel, not a room → "has a hidden tunnel cut 70 feet into the rock behind the heads" (Wikipedia: "about 70 feet", behind Lincoln). `real` dropped "Most of the rock was blasted off with dynamite" (the source gives no share) and now gives October 1927 to October 1941. The waist plan and the Jefferson move are confirmed (Wikipedia).
- th05 The White House: no change. Wikipedia: whitewashed when built, the burn-mark story is a myth; TR made "The White House" the formal name in 1901; the Residence has 132 rooms and 35 bathrooms. Wilson's sheep in World War I (WHHA).
- th06 The Liberty Bell: no change. Wikipedia: cracked when first rung after arriving in 1752; named "Liberty Bell" by abolitionists in the 1830s; "Pensylvania"; hidden under church floorboards in Allentown.
- th07 Chocolate: "was used as money" (it was the beans) → "is made from beans the ancient Maya used as money". "became a solid candy bar for the first time in England in 1847" → "got its first factory-made candy bars in England in 1847", because Nat Geo Kids credits Fry's with "the first mass-produced chocolate bars", not the first solid chocolate. Clay counterfeit beans, the Mayo-Chinchipe of Ecuador and the South American rainforest confirmed (Nat Geo Kids).
- th08 Dr. Seuss: the GRINCH plate rested on CBC alone → Springfield Museums (his 1985 Cadillac Seville; he and Audrey waited years for the GRINCH plate). CBC confirms the rest: Theodor Seuss Geisel, the 50-word bet with Bennett Cerf, an English degree at Oxford, and "Dr." for his father, who hoped he'd practice medicine.
- th09 Mickey Mouse: "voiced by Walt Disney himself until 1946" (D23 says 1946, but other accounts end it in 1947) → "for almost 20 years". `real` "His first color cartoon was The Band Concert" → "His cartoons didn't switch to color until The Band Concert in 1935" (D23 lists it as the first in color, but Mickey appeared in color in a 1932 promotional short). Walk of Fame Nov 13, 1978; Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho were started before Steamboat Willie (D23).
- th10 Mr. Potato Head: no change. Wikipedia: four votes, Boise, 1985; first toy advertised on TV, April 30, 1952; parts for a real potato; plastic body in 1964.
- th11 The Slinky: no change. Wikipedia: naval engineer, a spring knocked off a shelf in 1943, Vietnam radio antenna; Gimbels Philadelphia, Nov 27, 1945, 400 sold in 90 minutes (Wikipedia, Time); Betty James found the name in a dictionary (Time).
- th12 Crayola crayons: "made school chalk before it made crayons" is not shown by Crayola's own timeline, which puts its Staonal marking crayon and the dustless chalk both in 1902 → "came from a company that made red coloring for barn paint" (Crayola: Binney & Smith, 1885, "red oxide pigment used in barn paint"). Confirmed: eight for a nickel in 1903, named by Alice Binney, built-in sharpener in 1958 with the 64 box.
